The divisibility test

Tessellation means covering the plane with copies of a shape, with no gaps and no overlaps. If only one regular polygon is used, every vertex looks the same: the same number of copies must fit around a point.

The interior angle of a regular n-sided polygon is

the fraction (n minus 2) over n, times 180 degrees.

n − 2 n × 180°

Around a point those interior angles must add to exactly 360°. Equivalently, 360° divided by the interior angle must be an integer that is at least 3:

360 degrees divided by the interior angle equals the fraction 2n over (n minus 2).

360° interior angle = 2n n − 2

If the result is not an integer, a whole number of copies either leaves a gap or overlaps, so that regular polygon cannot tessellate the plane by itself. Only the equilateral triangle, the square, and the regular hexagon pass the test.
